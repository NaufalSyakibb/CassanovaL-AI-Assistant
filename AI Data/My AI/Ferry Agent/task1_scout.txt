## Landscape Summary
- **Definition & Scope**: Supply chain for micro businesses (usaha mikro) refers to the end-to-end flow of goods, information, and finance supporting micro-enterprises (typically <5 employees, informal or formal), often in low-capacity logistics environments. These businesses are central to emerging economies, especially in Southeast Asia.
- **Key Players**: Governments (e.g., Indonesia’s Ministry of Cooperatives and SMEs), multilateral agencies (World Bank, ILO, ADB), fintech platforms (e.g., Gojek, Tokopedia, Kredivo), cooperatives, and local NGOs. Academic centers like IPB University (Bogor Agricultural University) and Universitas Indonesia conduct applied research on MSME supply chains.
- **Major Debates**:
  - Digital inclusion vs. digital divide in enabling supply chain access for micro-entrepreneurs.
  - Last-mile logistics bottlenecks affecting perishable goods (e.g., agriculture, food).
  - Access to finance and working capital within fragmented supply chains.
  - Formalization of informal micro-enterprises to integrate into structured supply chains.
- **Timeline**:
  - 2010s: Rise of e-commerce and fintech enabling micro supply chain integration.
  - 2015–2020: Government programs (e.g., KUR credit, digital UMKM) and COVID-19 accelerated adoption of digital supply chain tools.
  - 2021–2025: Post-pandemic recovery, focus on resilience, sustainability, and green supply chains for MSMEs.
- **Regional Focus**: Indonesia leads in MSME policy innovation in ASEAN, with strong academic and policy literature in Indonesian and English.
- **Technology Trends**: Use of mobile apps, QRIS payments, cloud inventory tools, and AI-driven demand forecasting tailored for micro-scale.
- **Policy Instruments**: Subsidized credit, tax incentives, training programs, and infrastructure investment in rural logistics hubs.
