## WEAK / CONTESTED CLAIMS
[⚠] **Autocratic leadership is universally ineffective in modern organizations.**
| *Why weak*: While meta-analyses (e.g., Schyns & Schilling, 2013) highlight negative outcomes, Hannah et al. (2010) note its utility in crisis situations. The claim lacks nuance about contextual factors.
| *What would strengthen*: Empirical studies comparing autocratic leadership across industries (e.g., military vs. creative sectors) with longitudinal data.

[⚠] **Transformational leadership is always superior to transactional leadership.**
| *Why weak*: Wang et al. (2011) show transformational leadership’s advantages, but contingency theories (e.g., Fiedler’s model) suggest transactional leadership may outperform in stable, task-oriented environments. Overgeneralization risks bias.
| *What would strengthen*: Meta-analyses directly comparing both styles in controlled settings (e.g., lab experiments or matched field studies).

[⚠] **Autocratic leadership directly causes employee burnout.**
| *Why weak*: Schyns & Schilling (2013) find an *association*, but causality is unclear. Confounding variables (e.g., organizational culture, job demands) may mediate the relationship.
| *What would strengthen*: Longitudinal studies with control groups or experimental designs (e.g., leadership training interventions).

## CONTRADICTIONS
[↔] **Effectiveness of autocratic leadership in innovation:**
- *Rosing et al. (2011)*: Autocratic leadership is negatively associated with innovation.
- *Hannah et al. (2010)*: Autocratic leadership may be effective in *short-term* crisis situations (e.g., rapid decision-making).
| *Recommended resolution*: Distinguish between *short-term* (crisis) and *long-term* (innovation) contexts. Autocratic leadership may hinder creativity but enable swift execution in emergencies.

[↔] **Transformational leadership’s impact on team performance:**
- *Schaubroeck et al. (2007)*: Transformational leadership enhances team performance via team potency.
- *Wang et al. (2011)*: The effect size varies by industry (e.g., stronger in knowledge-work sectors).
| *Recommended resolution*: Acknowledge moderating variables (e.g., industry, team autonomy). Transformational leadership is not universally effective but excels in dynamic, collaborative environments.
